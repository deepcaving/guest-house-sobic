February*. Every post is a picture of the thing no competitor has: the
terrace, the fire, the water, the food.

## Captions — the first 9

Write like a person, not a brand. One line of scene-setting, one line of
substance, the number. The number is always in the caption, not only in the
comments.

## Reels — 1 a week, 15–30 seconds, vertical

1. The river running, from the terrace.
2. The fire being lit in the dining hall.
3. The cook putting a Pliva trout on the grill.
4. The terrace sofas at golden hour.
5. A guest casting on the river (get permission first).
6. The bedroom window opening onto the river in the morning.

Vertical. Caption in the caption, not burned in. Trending audio does nothing
for a lodge — the sound of the water does.

## Stories — 1–2 a week

- Behind the scenes in the kitchen.
- "Today's catch" — a guest's fish, with written permission.
- The view from the bedroom window in the morning.
- A close-up of a mayfly on the river. Fly-fishing accounts engage with
  these harder than with anything else.
- A poll: "May or October for your Pliva week?"
- A question sticker: "What is your favourite dry-fly?"

## The rule

Every post ends with the WhatsApp number. The link goes in the bio, not in the
caption. Nine times out of ten the first contact is a DM anyway — the number
is what you want in front of them.
