# TripAdvisor — claim and rebuild the listing

TripAdvisor is the slowest channel and the one that pays longest. It is where
a guest checks whether the property is real before they message, and where
the reviews live for years. Treat it as a three-month project, not a
three-day one.

**The listing:**
https://www.tripadvisor.com/Hotel_Review-g2319287-d7383736-Reviews-Guest_House_Sobic-Sipovo_Republika_Srpska.html

## 1. Claim it

1. Go to **tripadvisor.com/Owners** and search "Guest House Sobic".
2. Click **"Claim your listing"**.
3. Verify by phone — **TripAdvisor will call you**, and it must be a number
   that can receive calls. Use {{PHONE}} and have someone near the phone.
4. They may ask for documents: a business registration, a tax number, or
   proof of address. Keep them handy.
5. Verification can take days to weeks. Follow up, but calmly.

**If the listing cannot be claimed**, create a new one instead. Do not leave
it unclaimed — an unmanaged listing with old Booking.com-era photos is worse
than no listing, because it is the one a careful guest will open.

## 5. The archived reviews

There are reviews from the Booking.com era. **Respond to every single one**,
including the negative ones, and do it in the first week you have control.
Nothing signals "this place is looked after" like an owner who answers a
three-star review with actual care.

A good response:
- Thank them by name
- Say what specifically changed, if anything did
- Do not argue, do not explain the rating, do not apologise for the weather
- End with an invitation to come back

Example for a complaint about noise or heat:
> Thank you for taking the time to write this, [name] — and I'm sorry the room
> was too warm at night. We have replaced the linen and fitted a fan to every
> room since your stay, and the shutters now block the morning sun on the
> river side. It is a real problem in July and we've stopped it being one.
> We would be glad to have you back in the autumn, when the nights are cool
> and the BWO hatch is on. — [HOST], Pliva River Lodge

## 6. Getting the first reviews

| From | How |
|---|---|
| Past Booking.com guests | the email request in `email-templates.md` — 3–5 at a time, one at a time, never a BCC |
| Recent guests | 3–5 days after they leave, while they are still talking about it |
| A UK hosted-week guest | offer a 30% discount in exchange for a written trip report and a review |
| Trade press | the press-trip offer in `cold-dm-templates.md` |

**Ask, don't incentivise reviews on TripAdvisor.** Asking is fine. Paying or
offering a discount for a review is against their terms and against the law
in most places. A hosted-week discount for a *trip report* is fine; a
discount for a *TripAdvisor rating* is not.

## 7. Reply to every review, forever

Once a week, for the first year. Reviews are the ranking signal and the
replies are what the next guest actually reads. Set a recurring reminder —
this is the one marketing task that quietly compounds.
