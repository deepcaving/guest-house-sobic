## The post

Most fly fishers who have heard of Bosnia are thinking of May or June, or the
famous September and October BWO hatches on the Ribnik. They're right that
those are great months. They're missing November.

I spent the first week of November on the Pliva — a 33-km spring creek in the
north-west corner of Bosnia and Herzegovina, two kilometres below the Pliva
spring at Pljeva, downstream toward the town of Šipovo. The water comes out of
the ground at a constant 8 °C, gin-clear, with wild brown trout and trophy
grayling to 60 cm. Catch and release only. Dry fly heaven.

**What November gives you that the other months don't**

*The river to yourself.* The hosted weeks have ended. The German and UK groups
have gone home. The few anglers on the water are local, fishing on day
licences from the Šipovo club. You can wade a 2-km stretch and see nobody.

*The hatches keep going.* The autumn Blue-Winged Olives — Baetis rhodani and
Serratella ignita — are the headline event in September and October, but they
tail into November. Midges are active. The trout and grayling feed
confidently in the warmer part of the day, roughly 11 until 2.

*The light is extraordinary.* Late autumn in the Dinaric Alps means low sun,
long shadows, and the kind of golden hour that makes every photo look like a
stock image. The hills around the Pliva turn red and yellow. The river is
mirror-flat in the mornings.

*The water is low and clear.* This is the trade-off: November water is the
lowest of the year, which means you can see every fish, every stone, every
rise. Sight-fishing to a 50 cm grayling in 30 cm of gin-clear water, four
metres away from you, is what the Pliva is famous for — and November is when
it's most possible.

*The temperature is manageable.* Daytime highs in the Pliva valley in
November are 8–14 °C. Cold in the morning, warm enough at midday to wade in
shirtsleeves. A proper layering system and you're fine.

*The food is the reason to come back.* Our cook at the lodge makes soups from
scratch, wood-fire grilled meats for dinner, fresh trout when the day's catch
warrants it, and a breakfast of local honey, cheese, eggs and strong coffee
that stays with you until the wade-out.

*The lodge is on the bank.* Our seven rooms face the river. The dining hall
is 20 m from the water, with floor-to-ceiling windows. The terrace is over the
river. When you're not fishing, you're watching it.

*The price is right.* November is shoulder season. The hosted weeks at €1,790
are over. We're running 4-night short breaks from €830 per person, with a
guide, the licence, full board and the airport transfer from Sarajevo
included. The river is the same river as in May. The fishing is the same
fishing. The only difference is who's on it.

If you've already fished the Ribnik in October and you think you've seen
Bosnia — give November a try. The autumn is when the locals fish. There's a
reason for that.

## Why this post works

- It is a genuine argument, not a sales page. It has a thesis (November is
  underrated) and supports it with specifics — 8 °C, 30 cm of visibility,
  11 until 2, low water.
- It never says "book now". It says "give November a try" and leaves the link
  at the end.
- It mentions the price once, in the context of why November is cheaper.
  Once is enough.
- The name of the lodge and the town appear in the last paragraph, which is
  what a search engine indexes.
